# Native 60 fps investigation

_2026-10-17. Summary checked against the current code, with earlier experimental results from `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md` and `docs/HISTORY.md`. The experimental mode **already exists**; it was neither created nor validated in gameplay in this audit._

## Loop and 30 fps cap

The earlier mapping of `main` 1.0.0 identified the loop in `f_70000231a0`, the update in `f_700001d1c0` and the end of frame in `f_7000029d30` (`docs/PROGRESS.md`). `runtime/nvn/objects.c` records the interval requested by `nvnWindowSetPresentInterval`; `nvnQueuePresentTexture` calls `fps60_present()` and `present_pace()`. The game requests interval 2 of a 60 Hz vsync; by default the runtime waits ~33.33 ms. The experimental option forces ~16.67 ms **only** while the overworld updates; menu, battle and capture keep ~33.33 ms. `LARECOMP_FPS=60` on its own only changes the pacing and speeds up the logic (earlier measurement in `tools/repro.sh`).

The time step is not simply the VSync. The `G` table is at `main+0xf7eeb8` in Eevee 1.0.0, with `G[0]=16,666,667 ns` and `G[1]=33,333,334 ns` (`main+0xf7eec0`). `f_7000039600` copies the value into step 1 of the timer object; the overworld calls it on every update. `f_7000663660`/`f_7000664cd0`, `f_700067be50` and other consumers turn time into animation progress. Step 0 stays at ~33.3 ms and some subsystems only test whether it is positive. The full map of readers and addresses is in `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md`, "Time map".

The current 60 mode (`runtime/hle/fps60.c`) intercepts the accessor only during the overworld update, offering 16.67 ms to step 1 and restoring `G[1]` afterwards. `FIELD_UPDATE=0x70002f8bb0` tells the overworld apart from other scenes; `ENCOUNTER_SCENE=0x700028ef70` and `BATTLE_START=0x700028bb60` mark capture/battle. `runtime/hle/wildbattle.c` marks the wild battle through the alternative path. Pikachu 1.0.0 addresses are translated in `runtime/fnhook.c`; patches to the scene counters are generated by `tools/patches.py`, never edited by hand in `generated/`.

## Frame-count dependencies

Animations that use step 1 manage to keep their speed in the overworld at 60. Capture and battle timelines use integer counters; `tools/patches.py` modifies three stores for the mode 2 research (one tick every two updates). Menus/Pokédex also count frames: an opening that took 8 frames at 30 took 4 in native 60 mode, according to `docs/HISTORY.md` 47. The current mode keeps their logic at 30 and presents an intermediate frame; battle/capture stay at 30 without that frame because in-game tests exposed interpolation defects (`HISTORY.md` 44–49). Physics, particles, scripts, camera, audio and all battle timers have **not** been proven correct at 60 Hz.

## Alternatives

| Architecture | Possible gain | Dependencies and risk | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Simulation and rendering at 60 Hz | Each present gets a genuinely new scene; lower latency. | Requires mapping and adjusting every counter and system that assumes 30 ticks/s. Mode 2 is research, not proof of correctness. | Good long-term goal, high regression risk. |
| B. Simulation at 30 Hz, rendering at 60 | Original speed with the real pipeline redrawing intermediate frames. | NVN re-execution needs stable per-object identity, palettes, cuts and effects; GPU cost and latency. The earlier generic prototype was removed due to ghosting, wrong menus and stretched models. | Feasible per scene with game markers; there is no validated general solution. |
| C. Hybrid (current) | Overworld at 60 without doubling the speed; other scenes keep known behavior. | Transitions and interactions between systems need testing; menu quality depends on interpolation. | Best balance between fidelity and risk **in the current state**, still experimental. |

An intermediate frame in alternative B is a real draw from the pipeline, with interpolated state; it is not optical-flow generation. Even so, without identifying the same object in two frames, the image can be wrong. The mapping of `f_70006237b0`/`f_7000641190` found stable object pointers in part of the overworld (`docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md`), but it does not cover every scene.

## Public patch and compatibility

[Fl4sh9174's public repository](https://github.com/Fl4sh9174/Switch-Emulator-Ultrawide-FPS-Mods) contains mod packs for Eevee/Pikachu. The local decoding recorded in `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md` found, in the **1.0.2** patch, a write of `16,666,667` to `main+0xf7eec0` and a branch at `main+0x5e78b8`. The first matches `G[1]` of the **1.0.0** dump; the second **cannot be carried over by offset**: in 1.0.0 it is `blr x8`. The hypothesis that it changes the present interval still depends on the 1.0.2 binary. Changing `G[1]` globally speeds up readers in other scenes. The 120 FPS version writes ~10.7 ms, which does not even correspond to 1/120 s; there is no justification for applying it here.

[RT64](https://github.com/rt64/rt64/blob/main/include/rt64_extended_gbi.h) provides matrix group commands with IDs and interpolation options. This supports the direction of marking identity, but does not show that the N64 protocol can be copied to NVN. The local code and measurements take precedence over the claimed behavior of external mods.

## Prototype and validation status

The 10/15 draw re-execution prototype was removed; images and failures are in `docs/RESEARCH-60FPS.md`. The current hybrid mode is off by default (`fps = 30`), turns on with `fps = 60` or `LARECOMP_FPS60=1`, and keeps 30 in the critical scenes. `LARECOMP_FPS60=2` and `LARECOMP_FPS60_MENU_NATIVE=1` are research probes; a counter of 60 presents does **not** attest correct gameplay.

In this audit, two scripted **Eevee 1.0.0** runs reached the overworld with a copied save. In 60 mode, the log switched to `world: 60 fps` at present 1342; in presents 1360–1449, the intervals averaged 16.540 ms, P95 18.152 ms, P99 18.585 ms, with no frame above 50 ms. 30 mode averaged 33.334 ms over the same range of presents. The 60 mode screenshot showed the overworld, but **no comparison of gameplay position/timing, animation, capture, battle or audio was made**. The results only measure presentation in this short scene. Pikachu also still needs testing.

## Next technical steps

1. Instrument per-scene counters and clocks in Eevee/Pikachu, compare positions, animations, turns, capture and audio at the same seconds of game time at 30/60.
2. Show whether other threads read `G[1]` during the hook; replace the transient global write with a safe local modification if the race exists.
3. Extend the stable identity of the objects that record command lists. Re-execute one scene at a time, with tests of cuts, bones, shadows, UI, particles and object spawn/despawn.
4. Only promote battle/capture/menus to native 60 Hz after fixing all relevant counters and validating gameplay, not just the visual duration.
